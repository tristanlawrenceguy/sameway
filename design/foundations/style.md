# Style: paper and ink

What the product looks like, in words, so the assistant and every other
reader build the same thing.

**Paper.** Warm near-white, never grey, never pure white except for a
raised surface. Nothing loads from a network: the type is the platform's
own serif for titles and sans for reading.

**Ink.** Text is ink-dark, not black. Titles are set in the serif, at the
top of the scale, with air under them. A title reads as a title before its
size is noticed.

**Two colours.** Indigo is the person; teal is the assistant. They meet in
the brand mark and in the line under the masthead. The person's colour
washes the top of every page, because the page is theirs, and it is the
colour of links, of the current place in the navigation, of a selection,
and of what they say in the conversation. The assistant's colour marks
what it says and what it made. No third colour is used for decoration.

**Colour says something.** Beyond those two, colour is a state: green is
done, blue is a state a thing is in, amber is a day that has passed or a
notice from the software, red is an error. Every one of those is also
said in words; the colour is the second telling, never the only one.

**Facts are chips.** What a person wants to know about a record at a
glance, its state, whether it is done, the day it is due, is a chip under
its title, in a colour that says the same thing the words do. A timestamp
is not a chip; it is a quiet line.

**Air.** Regions are separated by space, not boxes: a listing is rows with
a hairline between them, a record's fields are rows, a page head has room
under it. Boxes are for things that are one thing: a block on the canvas,
a message, a panel. Hairlines are soft; borders are for controls.

**Rounded, not round.** Surfaces have a large radius; controls are pills.
Nothing is a sharp rectangle except a table.

**Motion explains.** See [motion.md](motion.md). Nothing moves to look
alive.
